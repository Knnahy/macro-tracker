Macro & Nutrient Reference Guide

General nutritional information only; not medical diagnosis or treatment advice.

Protein (4 kcal/g)

Provides structural nitrogen and amino acids for tissue repair, enzymes, hormones, and immune synthesis. Dietary protein supplies nine essential amino acids that the human body cannot synthesize endogenously:

  • Histidine: Precursor to histamine; supports myelin maintenance and immune signaling.
  • Isoleucine: Branched-chain amino acid involved in muscle metabolism and hemoglobin synthesis.
  • Leucine: Primary trigger for skeletal muscle protein synthesis via mTOR signaling.
  • Lysine: Required for collagen cross-linking, calcium absorption, and carnitine production.
  • Methionine: Sulfur donor essential for methylation pathways, glutathione, and tissue growth.
  • Phenylalanine: Precursor for tyrosine and catecholamine neurotransmitters (dopamine, norepinephrine).
  • Threonine: Principal structural component of mucin glycoproteins in gut barrier and enamel.
  • Tryptophan: Precursor for serotonin, melatonin, and endogenous niacin synthesis.
  • Valine: Branched-chain amino acid supporting nitrogen balance and muscular energy supply.

Carbohydrates (4 kcal/g)

Primary substrate for high-intensity muscular work and central nervous system ATP production. Stored as hepatic and muscular glycogen to regulate blood glucose and spare dietary protein from gluconeogenesis.

Fat (9 kcal/g)

Dense energy reserve, structural component of phospholipid cell membranes, and vehicle for fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K). Supplies essential fatty acids required for eicosanoid and steroid hormone synthesis.

Dietary Fiber (~0–2 kcal/g)

Non-digestible plant polysaccharides and lignin. Soluble fractions modulate gastric emptying and glycemic response; insoluble fractions increase fecal bulk and support colonic transit and short-chain fatty acid fermentation.

Alcohol / Ethanol (7 kcal/g)

Non-essential energy substrate prioritized for hepatic oxidation via alcohol dehydrogenase. Temporarily suppresses lipid oxidation and provides energy without contributing structural macronutrients or micronutrients.
